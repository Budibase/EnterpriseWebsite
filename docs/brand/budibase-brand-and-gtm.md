# Budibase brand and GTM direction

**Status:** Working source of truth  
**Last updated:** 16 October 2026

Use this document to guide Budibase website design, customer-facing copy,
campaigns, presentations, customer stories, sales enablement, product launches,
and other go-to-market communications.

## Mission

Make critical work move faster.

This is the approved company mission. Use this wording when stating Budibase's
mission in current customer-facing content.

## Strategic objective

Make Budibase the open-source platform enterprise IT uses to build and run the
software behind business operations.

The wider organization continually needs new applications, workflows,
integrations, automations, and AI capabilities. IT is accountable for delivering
them while maintaining security, governance, reliability, architecture, and
cost. Demand for this software is greater than IT's capacity to deliver it.

Budibase gives IT another option:

> Deliver more of what the business needs, faster, without giving up control.

The strategic outcome is not merely greater developer productivity. It is more
leverage for enterprise IT to create business value across the organization.

## Strategy hierarchy

Keep this hierarchy intact across brand, product, and GTM work:

- **Business outcome:** Deliver more business value.
- **Customer outcome:** Give IT the leverage to deliver more.
- **Functional promise:** Build and run more of the software the organization
  needs.
- **Emotional payoff:** Say yes to more.
- **Enterprise reassurance:** Without losing control.
- **Category:** The open-source platform for enterprise operations.
- **Brand expression:** Build freely. Operate with control.

The simplest description of the direction is:

> Budibase gives enterprise IT the leverage to build and run more of the
> software the business needs, without giving up control.

Treat “Say yes to more” and “Build freely. Operate with control.” as strategic
expressions rather than approved public taglines until explicitly adopted.

## Audience model

### Primary customer and platform owner

Enterprise IT is the primary customer and accountable owner. Relevant roles
include CIOs, CTOs, heads of IT, enterprise architects, platform teams,
application-development leaders, business-systems teams, and technical teams
responsible for internal software and automation.

### Builders

The default builder is an IT practitioner or a technical builder working within
IT governance. Do not frame independent citizen development or an operations
team building outside IT as the primary adoption model unless customer evidence
supports it.

### Use-case owners

Procurement, finance, manufacturing, HR, compliance, operations, IT, and other
functions own the business processes and create demand for change. They provide
requirements, operational context, sponsorship, and outcome evidence.

### End users

Teams across the organization use the applications, workflows, agents, and
interfaces that IT delivers. They should receive appropriate access and actions
without needing broad access to underlying systems, credentials, or data stores.

### Buying and assurance stakeholders

- CIOs, CTOs, heads of IT, and business executives accountable for change and
  operational continuity.
- Security, compliance, risk, procurement, architecture, and data-governance
  stakeholders.
- Functional leaders accountable for the process and its business outcome.

### Priority environments

Enterprise IT is the customer category. Regulated, security-conscious,
operationally complex, and mission-critical organizations remain the priority
go-to-market environment, including financial services, government, defence,
aerospace, manufacturing, healthcare, pharmaceuticals, and critical
infrastructure.

This is a market wedge, not a claim that only regulated organizations can use
Budibase.

## Partner GTM

Delivery partnerships are a core route to market for Budibase Enterprise.
Consultancies, systems integrators, agencies, and service providers extend the
delivery capacity, specialist expertise, and regional reach available to
customers. They help enterprise IT discover, design, build, integrate, deploy,
and support operational software with Budibase.

The partner model must preserve the customer-accountability model: enterprise
IT remains the platform owner and retains responsibility for architecture,
security, governance, data, deployment, and operations. Partners add delivery
leverage around that ownership rather than displacing it.

The public proposition is the **Budibase partner network**. Its website
directory distinguishes two partner types: **Technology** and **Service**.
Technology partners show **NA** for partner tier in the directory.
Approved Service partner tiers are **Gold** for large agencies, **Silver** for
small agencies, and **Bronze** for individuals. These assignments do not imply
certification or additional commercial benefits.
Its website experience serves two connected jobs:

- Help enterprise buyers find delivery partners by region and partnership tier.
- Help qualified delivery organizations explore and join the network.

While the network is early, emphasize product resources, technical guidance,
joint delivery planning, and selective co-marketing. Only use approved tier
assignments. Do not imply certification, guaranteed leads, revenue sharing,
exclusive access, or fixed commercial benefits unless those programs are
approved and operational.

## Customer problem

Enterprise IT faces an unattractive set of choices when the business requests
another operational system:

- Custom development is slow, expensive, and difficult to maintain at the
  volume the business requires.
- Packaged software rarely matches every operational process and is slow to
  adapt.
- Adding another SaaS product fragments data, ownership, identity, approvals,
  and operational context.
- Uncontrolled low-code and AI create shadow IT, governance, security, and
  maintenance risk.
- Engineering backlogs force IT to delay valuable work or say no.

Budibase should present a credible alternative: a governed, extensible platform
that increases IT delivery capacity while keeping ownership, security,
governance, and operations with IT.

### Recurring customer pattern

A strong recurring use case is private enterprise data made useful through a
secure, purpose-built interface. IT connects an existing database, API, or
system and creates a web app or agent through which an end user can request,
view, or act on information. Permissions, approvals, and human oversight are
applied where required.

This pattern is important product proof, especially for Connect and Control. It
is not the entire category or the master company proposition.

## Positioning

### Category

The open-source platform for enterprise operations.

“Enterprise operations” describes the work Budibase powers. It does not mean
Budibase targets a department called Operations. Every function has operational
processes, and enterprise IT provides and governs the software behind them.

### Working positioning statement

For enterprise IT teams facing more software demand than they can deliver,
Budibase is the open-source platform for enterprise operations. It brings apps,
automations, agents, data, and APIs together around existing enterprise systems,
giving IT the leverage to build and run more of the software the organization
needs without losing ownership, governance, or control.

### Core proposition

Build the software your organization needs, faster, without losing control.

### Brand idea

Build freely. Operate with control.

Budibase should give IT substantially more freedom to build without requiring
it to sacrifice the controls expected of enterprise software. Open source,
self-hosting, permissions, approval policies, auditability, model choice, and
deployment flexibility are manifestations of the same underlying value:
ownership and control.

Do not try to make Budibase visually “look open source.” Demonstrate that the
customer remains in control.

### Emotional proposition

Say yes to more.

When the business requests another application, workflow, integration,
automation, or AI capability, IT traditionally has to build custom software,
buy another product, add the work to a backlog, tolerate shadow IT, or say no.
Budibase creates another answer: yes, we can deliver that.

The emotional outcome only works when it is supported by delivery. Budibase
should help IT move from being perceived as a constraint on business change to
being recognized as an engine for it.

## Product model

### Build

Give IT flexible primitives rather than prescribing how every process should
work:

- **Apps** for human interaction.
- **Automations** for predictable, repeatable processes.
- **Agents** for context-dependent work that benefits from reasoning.
- **Data and APIs** to connect the system.

These should increasingly work as one system rather than being presented as
independent products.

### Connect

Budibase should work with the enterprise estate rather than trying to replace
it. SAP, Microsoft, Oracle, Salesforce, ServiceNow, SQL databases, APIs,
internal systems, and future AI infrastructure should be systems Budibase can
orchestrate around.

> Keep your systems. Keep your data. Build around them with Budibase.

Integration depth matters more than connector count. Show what data can be
read, what actions can be performed, which identity executes them, and how the
result is governed.

### Control

Enterprise IT must remain responsible for what happens. The platform story
should make these controls visible where supported:

- Identity and access.
- Roles and permissions.
- Approval policies and human oversight.
- Agent permissions and execution identity.
- Credentials and model choice.
- Auditability.
- Deployment and data location.
- Publishing, release, and change governance.

AI should operate within the organization's rules rather than outside them.

### Operate

Building software is not enough. IT needs to understand what happens after it
is published:

- What is running?
- Who is using it?
- What actions are happening?
- What requires approval?
- What failed?
- What are agents doing?
- Which systems are being touched?
- Who owns the solution?

Activity, requests, actions, approvals, automation runs, and agent execution
should increasingly contribute to an operational control plane across Budibase.

### Prove

Budibase should help IT demonstrate the business value it delivers. Move from
counting assets created toward showing operational outcomes such as requests
processed, actions completed, completion rates, approval time, manual effort
removed, reliability, and adoption.

Any metrics used in marketing must come from supported product data or approved
customer evidence. Illustrative figures are not claims and must be labelled as
examples in internal work.

### Product direction and current truth

Build, Connect, Control, Operate, and Prove describe the intended platform
system. They do not imply that every capability is equally mature today.
Customer-facing work must distinguish current capabilities from roadmap
direction.

Projects could eventually represent the operational solutions IT delivers,
including their apps, agents, automations, connections, approval policies,
ownership, portability, deployment, and lifecycle. This remains an exploratory
direction rather than a settled architecture.

Budibase already has workspace-level publishing, release history, and restore.
Customers have also requested Git. Do not advocate conventional version control
only because enterprise software is expected to have it. Understand the
underlying requirements: external source control, diffs, review, CI/CD,
environment promotion, auditability, configuration as code, and integration
with existing enterprise software-delivery processes.

The strategic requirement is:

> Budibase must fit naturally into how enterprise IT delivers software.

## Differentiators

Emphasize the differentiators relevant to the audience and task:

1. **IT leverage.** Deliver more operational software with the people and
   resources already available.
2. **One operational platform.** Apps, automations, agents, data, APIs, and
   human oversight work together.
3. **Built and run in one place.** Support the lifecycle of an operation, not
   only prototyping or interface creation.
4. **Works around the existing estate.** Connect and orchestrate existing
   systems rather than demanding wholesale replacement.
5. **Open and extensible.** Open-source foundations, plugins, APIs,
   connections, and model choice reduce lock-in.
6. **Deploy on your terms.** Support the environments required by architecture,
   policy, sovereignty, and risk.
7. **Enterprise control.** Access, governance, auditability, security, and
   release discipline belong in the core platform story.
8. **Human oversight for AI.** Agents operate within permissions, policies,
   approvals, and accountable operational processes.
9. **Governed interfaces for private data.** End users can request the data and
   actions they need without being given broad access to underlying systems.
10. **Designed for consequential work.** Show requests, approvals, cases,
    incidents, assets, audits, inspections, and other real workflows.

## Competitive landscape

**Working view as of 22 August 2026.** This is strategic guidance, not a claim
about market share. Revalidate it as competitors and customer evaluations
change.

### Biggest overall competitor: Microsoft Power Platform

Microsoft Power Platform is the biggest practical competitive threat. It
combines applications, process automation, agents, data, connectors, developer
extension points, environment management, and enterprise governance. Its place
inside the Microsoft estate can make it the default answer before a formal
evaluation begins.

Do not compete by claiming that Budibase has more pieces. Win where Microsoft
creates friction: ownership, deployment choice, understandable architecture,
cross-estate neutrality, cost clarity, depth around private systems, and a more
coherent builder experience.

### Strategic incumbent: ServiceNow

ServiceNow is the closest established analogue for enterprise IT digitizing and
automating how the organization operates. Its App Engine combines workflow
applications, agents, data, governance, security, and change management on the
ServiceNow platform.

Budibase should not try to become another closed system of record. The contrast
is:

> Keep your systems, data, and infrastructure. Build the operational layer
> around them with Budibase.

### Direct enterprise platform competitors

- **OutSystems and Mendix:** Mature enterprise application platforms moving
  aggressively toward governed apps, agents, workflows, deployment, and
  lifecycle management.
- **Appian:** Strong process-orchestration proposition connecting people,
  systems, automation, and agents in complex regulated work.
- **Retool:** A close builder-level alternative with apps, workflows, agents,
  self-hosting, administration, and permissions, particularly for engineering,
  data, and operations teams.

These brands are converging on speed plus governance. “Faster with control” is
not sufficient differentiation on its own. Budibase must make open ownership,
deployment flexibility, cross-estate fit, product accessibility, and the whole
Build–Connect–Control–Operate–Prove system tangible.

### Alternatives outside the formal category

Custom development, packaged SaaS, internal platform frameworks, automation
tools, point AI products, and simply leaving the request in the backlog are all
competitors. GTM work should explain why Budibase is preferable to the
customer's current delivery model, not only why it is different from another
low-code vendor.

## Winning the hearts and minds of IT

Winning enterprise IT requires more than satisfying a security checklist. The
brand, product, documentation, community, sales process, and customer evidence
must respect IT's responsibility and make practitioners feel more capable.

### Respect the job

Speak to IT as the team accountable for turning business demand into dependable
software. Do not imply that professional development, architecture, security,
or governance are obstacles to be bypassed. Avoid “replace developers,”
“democratize development,” and citizen-developer-first narratives.

### Make IT the hero

Celebrate the team that made a critical process work, not only the interface
that was built. Show IT saying yes, shipping quickly, keeping control, and
earning recognition for the result.

### Deliver an early, credible win

The first experience should make it possible to connect a real system, build a
useful workflow, apply permissions, publish safely, and see activity without a
long platform program. Fast time to value earns attention; a credible path to
governance earns adoption.

### Fit the existing estate

Show architecture, network paths, identity, credentials, execution context,
data movement, failure behavior, and deployment options. Make deep connections
to existing systems more important than a decorative connector catalogue.

### Make control a product experience

Permissions, approvals, environments, publishing, audit history, activity,
agent actions, and ownership should be visible in product demonstrations and
core navigation. Trust should not be confined to a security page or sales
appendix.

### Fit the software-delivery lifecycle

Help IT understand how Budibase supports review, testing, promotion, rollback,
change history, ownership, reuse, and integration with existing SDLC practices.
Be precise about what exists now and transparent about gaps.

### Earn trust through technical transparency

Invest in excellent documentation, reference architectures, security and
deployment guidance, release notes, status communication, migration guidance,
and clear product limits. Explain how the system behaves when it succeeds and
when it fails.

### Preserve practitioner agency

Open source, APIs, plugins, self-hosting, model choice, and exportability should
feel like practical control rather than ideology. Provide escape hatches and
extension points. Let technical users inspect, adapt, and integrate the system.

### Build a practitioner movement

Give IT builders reusable operational templates, architecture patterns,
implementation guides, community recognition, and direct access to product
expertise. Encourage practitioners to share how they solved the operational
problem, governed it, and measured the result.

### Help champions sell upward

Provide business-case tools, architecture packs, security answers, evaluation
plans, total-cost framing, and outcome dashboards. A practitioner should be able
to explain both why Budibase is useful and why the organization can trust it.

### Prove value after launch

Help IT show what the platform is doing for the business: work completed,
requests processed, time saved, reliability, adoption, and controlled AI
actions. The goal is to make IT's contribution visible without inventing or
overgeneralizing metrics.

## Proof and evidence

Known customer context includes the U.S. Space Force, American Express, and
Saab. Together they signal relevance to government and defence, regulated
financial services, and complex aerospace and engineering environments.

The common pattern reported across these customers is private data made
available to end users through a secure, purpose-built interface, which can be
a web app or an agent and can include a request-and-approval mechanism. Treat
this as an important product pattern, not the full company proposition.

Before publication, confirm the approved public form of every customer name,
logo, quotation, metric, case study, and relationship claim. Do not infer that
a logo is approved because it exists in an asset library or draft page.

Prefer evidence that explains:

- The operational problem and why it mattered.
- What IT delivered and how quickly it reached useful operation.
- Which existing systems, data, and people were connected.
- Why governance, security, deployment, or extensibility mattered.
- How the software was operated after launch.
- The measurable or observable business result.

A smaller amount of meaningful proof is stronger than an unexplained logo wall.

## Brand personality

Budibase is:

- **Capable:** Demonstrate how the system works and support claims with proof.
- **Direct:** Lead with the operational point and use concrete language.
- **Approachable:** Make serious software understandable without making it feel
  lightweight.
- **Controlled:** Show ownership, predictability, governance, and oversight.
- **Modern:** Be technically current without chasing AI spectacle or visual
  fashion.
- **Practical:** Show real workflows, constraints, decisions, and outcomes.
- **Bold:** Make strong, memorable statements where evidence supports them.
- **Open:** Reinforce ownership, extensibility, choice, and interoperability.

Budibase is not:

- A playful no-code product or citizen-development platform.
- A developer infrastructure tool disconnected from business outcomes.
- Heavyweight legacy enterprise software.
- A generic workplace productivity suite.
- Another AI company built around autonomous-agent hype.

The seriousness should come primarily from what Budibase is shown doing, not
from making the identity dark, severe, or intimidating. The brand can remain
bold, green, colorful, and approachable while showing consequential software.

> Serious software does not have to feel complicated.

## Design principles

### Built with precision

Use disciplined grids, clear hierarchy, deliberate spacing, concise copy, and
well-structured technical information. The experience should feel engineered.

### Bold, never noisy

Use bold notched typography and strong brand color for decisive moments.
Create emphasis through scale and restraint. Decorative variety must not
compete with meaning.

### Show work moving through the system

Do not merely illustrate software. Show a request entering, policy being
checked, an agent or automation acting, a person approving, an enterprise
system updating, and the action being recorded.

Budibase's raw visual material is work flowing through an organization:

> People → requests → apps → agents → policies → approvals → automations →
> enterprise systems → actions

Prefer product interfaces, workflow sequences, architecture diagrams, data
flows, approvals, audit trails, operational states, and outcomes over abstract
decoration.

### Make control visible

Security, governance, permissions, deployment, activity, release discipline,
and human oversight should appear throughout the story. Do not isolate trust on
a single security page.

### Make consequence visible without becoming severe

Show purchase requests, supplier onboarding, manufacturing work, audits,
incidents, inspections, access requests, and other consequential workflows.
Avoid adopting the dark, militarized, or intimidating aesthetic often used to
signal enterprise seriousness.

### Earn enterprise confidence with evidence

Use customer outcomes, product detail, architecture, standards, and specific
capabilities. Conservative styling alone is not evidence of maturity.

## Visual direction

### Color

- Use five core colors: Lime (`--bb-lime`, `#A5FF00`), Olive (`--bb-olive`,
  `#233207`), Coconut (`--bb-coconut`, `#F7F5F2`), Sand (`--bb-sand`, `#F4F2F0`),
  and Graphite (`--bb-graphite`, `#1E1919`). Keep Olive as the primary dark brand
  anchor and Lime for action, progress, connection, and successful states.
- Keep the names **Lime** and **Olive**. “Electric green” describes Lime's vivid
  character rather than a separate color or token.
- Retain the Lime and Olive 100–1600 families for pale treatments and interaction
  states. Their core aliases reference lime 300 and olive 1300 respectively.
- The current website trial uses white for light marketing surfaces, raised
  panels, and product interfaces, with Coconut for cards and supporting panels.
  Sand remains an additional warm neutral. Body copy uses
  Graphite, brand headings and links use Olive, subtle text uses grey 600,
  decorative borders use grey 200, and strong control boundaries use grey 500.
- Use the exact named accents and 50–1000 warm-grey scale from
  [Dropbox's color suite](https://brand.dropbox.com/color). Accent pairs are
  Azalea/Pink, Crimson/Sunset, Rust/Tangerine, Gold/Vivid Vargas, Ocean/Zen,
  Navy/Cloud, and Plum/Orchid. Do not import Dropbox Blue, Canopy, or Dropbox Lime.
  Named anchors are exact; pale supporting surfaces may mix a light accent at
  10% or 20% with Coconut. Choose readable foregrounds rather than assuming every
  accent supports the same text color.
- Shared shapes pair Olive backgrounds with Lime foregrounds and reverse this
  for inverted marks. The homepage announcement is trialling bright backgrounds with dark shapes:
  Orange (`#FF5E00`)/Umber (`#38271C`), Cyan (`#20DFF0`)/Petrol (`#233438`), and
  Violet (`#B89AFF`)/muted Plum (`#2E2B35`). These are page-scoped trial values; the existing
  global accent anchors remain unchanged. Navigation status lights use olive 1000. Asset symbols use Agent/Zen, App/Tangerine, Automation/Orchid, Operations
  center/Olive, Table/Vivid Vargas, and Function/Lime, with Graphite foregrounds
  on light fills and Coconut on Olive.
- The global footer uses Olive with Lime headings and Coconut links.
- Primary buttons generally use full Lime fills with Olive text
  and shared Lime interaction tokens. The Cloud pricing trial button and
  Book a demo in light-page navigation use Olive with Coconut text and
  darker Olive interaction states. Dark homepage navigation retains Lime. Secondary and outlined actions use white
  fills, Olive text, and grey-800 outlines, including Try Budibase on dark
  navigation surfaces. Keep the 10px cut corner and use bold arrow icons.
  Trailing arrows sit in small tiles with a 4px top-right notch: Olive with
  Coconut arrows, or white with Olive arrows on Olive buttons. Standard
  buttons keep equal top, bottom and right tile insets. Apply this treatment
  to platform landing-page hero, feature and closing CTAs. Product-demo
  controls use native pill-shaped buttons with sentence-case labels and
  compact interface sizing; keep website button notches and arrow tiles
  outside the product interfaces.
  Use Vivid Vargas and Gold for proof, selection, attention, and human review;
  use Crimson for danger. Do not use Lime as a full FeatureSection background.
- Keep accents meaningful to the operational story. Avoid rainbow palettes
  without meaning, novelty gradients, and excessive variation across sections.

  closing CTA actions. These display statements remain uppercase at weight `700`.
- Use **Stack Sans Text** for H2–H6 headings, section and card titles, labels,
  buttons, body copy, navigation, descriptions, technical values, and product
  interfaces. The large closing CTA actions use Notch; keep supporting headings
  in sentence case.
- Use weight `300` for regular body copy and supporting text, and `400` for
  navigation, section headings, and supporting labels. Action-button labels
  use `500`. The heavier Stack Sans
  forms replace the previous `400`/`500` roles with `300`/`400` respectively;
  retain existing semibold and bold emphasis, including `700` hero headlines.
- Use each face at its native width. Do not horizontally compress headlines.
- Keep shared section sizes at 42px desktop, 36px tablet, and 30px mobile.
  Shared hero tokens use 64px desktop, 52px tablet, and a fluid 36–48px mobile
  scale. The split homepage hero uses a fluid 36–52px desktop size to keep
  “Make critical work” above “move faster.” within its copy column.
- Highlight the homepage's “MOVE FASTER.” line with a tight Lime background,
  Olive text, and the same 10px top-right corner cut as the buttons.
- Style action-button labels in uppercase Text at weight `500`, with bold
  arrows. Desktop navigation buttons are 32px tall with 14px labels and 16px arrows; retain
  at least 44px touch targets in mobile navigation.
- Retain monospace for code. Technical identifiers may use tabular numerals
  and a slashed zero; do not request stylistic sets from a previous typeface.
- Reserve the strongest uppercase treatment for hero statements and actions.
  Keep supporting copy readable and layouts focused on operational meaning.
- Set navigation dropdown group headers in uppercase on desktop and mobile.

  product visuals, and FAQs.

  evidence sections.
- Use rounded cards and pills only where they have a functional reason. Avoid a
  soft consumer-SaaS appearance.
- Use square status lights throughout the homepage, including section markers
  and status indicators in its product demonstrations.
- Give desktop and mobile navigation dropdown links uniform olive-1000 8px

  community foundation for Community, enterprise framework for Enterprise,

## Reference brands

Use references as specific lenses, not templates.

### Primary references

- **Samsara — operational visibility:** Learn from how Samsara derives its
  story from the operational world its customers inhabit and makes software
  feel consequential by showing work, assets, events, and outcomes.
- **GitLab — ownership and speed with control:** Learn from how open source,
  self-management, enterprise governance, policy, deployment choice, and a
  unified lifecycle coexist without splitting the brand.
- **Atlassian — platform accessibility:** Learn from how a broad enterprise
  platform is organized and made approachable to practitioners and leaders.

### Supporting references

- **Cloudflare — technical approachability:** Learn from its unusually clear
  documentation, architecture explanations, self-service adoption, and ability
  to make powerful infrastructure feel direct and operable.
- **Datadog — live operational visibility:** Learn from how it makes complex
  system state, ownership, activity, and action visible across a broad platform.
- **Grafana — practitioner credibility:** Retain selectively for open-source-to-
  enterprise progression, technical evidence, extensibility, and community
  trust.

### Selective references

- **Palantir — consequence:** Borrow the sense that software affects real
  operations, not the severity, opacity, or aesthetic.
- **Rippling — commercial discipline:** Learn from its ability to present a
  complex operational platform as a coherent system, without adopting glossy
  lifestyle-led art direction.

ServiceNow and Microsoft Power Platform should be studied as competitors and
category shapers, not used as visual templates.

The intended combination is:

> Samsara's operational visibility + GitLab's ownership and control +
> Atlassian's accessibility + Budibase's distinctive green, open identity.

## Messaging system

### Message hierarchy

Organize customer-facing narratives in this order when appropriate:

1. The business or operational outcome IT needs to deliver.
2. Why demand exceeds the current delivery model.
3. How Budibase combines apps, automations, agents, data, and APIs.
4. How Budibase works around existing systems.
5. How IT retains control through governance, deployment, ownership, and
   oversight.
6. How the system is operated and its value made visible.
7. Evidence from the product, architecture, customers, or measured outcomes.
8. A clear next action.

### Preferred language

Use language such as:

- Give IT more leverage.
- Build and run the software behind business operations.
- Deliver more of what the business needs.
- Build freely. Operate with control.
- Keep your systems and data.
- Connect the existing enterprise estate.
- Ownership, governance, auditability, and human oversight.
- Deploy on your terms.
- Apps, automations, agents, data, and APIs working together.
- Reliable, accountable, and production-ready.

### Language to handle carefully

- **No-code/low-code:** Use for clarity or search intent, not as the primary
  enterprise identity.
- **Citizen developer:** Do not make this the default builder or adoption
  model.
- **AI-powered/AI-first:** Explain the operational job, permissions, controls,
  and outcome instead.
- **Build anything:** Replace with consequential operational examples.
- **Democratize:** State who can do what and under which controls.
- **Magic, effortless, revolutionary:** Replace hype with evidence.
- **Digital transformation:** Name the actual operational change.

### Voice

- Write short, declarative headlines.
- Use precise nouns and active verbs.
- Respect IT's technical knowledge and accountability.
- Explain complex capabilities plainly without removing substance.
- Prefer confidence over superlatives.
- Match every major claim with product detail, architecture, customer proof, or
  another credible reason to believe.

## Guardrails

- Do not fabricate or overstate customer relationships, compliance status,
  certifications, performance, security guarantees, or product capabilities.
- Distinguish current capabilities from roadmap direction.
- Do not use regulated-enterprise anxiety as fear-based marketing.
- Do not position operations teams or citizen developers working independently
  of IT as the default model.
- Do not imply that Budibase replaces the enterprise estate.
- Do not remove the open-source identity in pursuit of enterprise seriousness.
- Do not make the brand so conservative that it becomes interchangeable with
  legacy enterprise software.
- Do not let AI spectacle or visual novelty obscure product understanding.
- Do not publish illustrative operational metrics as real evidence.
