# Budibase brand and GTM direction

**Status:** Working source of truth  
**Last updated:** 21 August 2026

Use this document to guide Budibase website design, customer-facing copy,
campaigns, presentations, customer stories, sales enablement, product launches,
and other go-to-market communications.

## Strategic objective

Move Budibase upmarket by making it the credible platform for operations and IT
teams in regulated enterprises to build and run critical operations.

Budibase must communicate three qualities at the same time:

- IT and technical teams can connect private enterprise data and rapidly create
  governed interfaces around it, including web apps and agents.
- End users can request data or actions through those interfaces, with
  permissions, approvals, and human oversight applied where required, instead
  of receiving broad access to underlying systems or data stores.
- IT retains the control, security, governance, reliability, and deployment
  flexibility required for critical enterprise operations.

This access-and-control tension is the center of the brand:

> Make private data useful without giving up control.

Treat this as a strategic expression, not an approved public tagline unless it
is explicitly adopted.

## Audience

### Primary practitioners

- Operations leaders and teams responsible for essential business processes.
- IT leaders, platform teams, enterprise architects, and technical teams that
  enable and govern internal systems.
- Builders inside IT and operations who create apps, forms, workflows,
  automations, integrations, and AI-assisted processes.

### Buying and assurance stakeholders

- CIOs, CTOs, heads of IT, and heads of operations.
- Security, compliance, risk, procurement, and data-governance stakeholders.
- Business executives accountable for operational continuity and improvement.

### Priority environments

Regulated, security-conscious, operationally complex, or mission-critical
organizations, including financial services, government, defence, aerospace,
manufacturing, healthcare, pharmaceuticals, and critical infrastructure.

## Customer problem

Regulated enterprises often face an unattractive choice:

- Packaged software is slow to adapt and rarely matches their exact processes.
- Custom software is expensive, slow to deliver, and difficult to maintain.
- Lightweight no-code tools can create governance, security, and scale concerns.
- Point solutions fragment data, ownership, approvals, and operational context.
- AI without oversight introduces risk rather than dependable operational value.

A recurring customer need is more specific: valuable operational data is held
in private databases and systems, but the people responsible for day-to-day
work need a secure, purpose-built way to request it, view it, or act on it. The
interface may be a web app or an agent, and the interaction often includes a
request-and-approval mechanism. Giving every end user direct access to the
source system is inappropriate, while building and maintaining a custom
interface for each process is slow and expensive.

Budibase should present a credible alternative: a governed, extensible platform
that lets IT and technical teams create governed web apps and agents over
private data, giving end users appropriate access and actions through
permissions, requests, approvals, and oversight while the organization retains
control.

## Positioning

### Category

An open-source platform for enterprise operations.

### Working positioning statement

For operations and IT teams in regulated enterprises, Budibase is the
open-source platform for building and running critical operations. It brings
private data, secure web apps, automations, integrations, AI agents, and human
oversight together so end users can request information and complete
operational work without exposing underlying systems or sacrificing enterprise
control.

### Core promise

Turn private enterprise data into governed web apps, agents, and workflows
without giving up security, governance, reliability, or ownership.

### Brand shorthand

Budibase should feel like operational infrastructure: capable, adaptable,
controlled, and trusted with important work.

## Differentiators

Emphasize these differentiators according to the task and audience:

1. **Governed interfaces for private data.** Give end users purpose-built web
   apps or agents through which they can request the data and actions they need
   without exposing underlying systems.
2. **One operational platform.** Apps, forms, automations, agents, data, APIs,
   integrations, and human oversight work together.
3. **Built and run in one place.** Budibase supports the lifecycle of an
   operation, not only rapid prototyping or interface creation.
4. **Open and extensible.** Open-source foundations, plugins, APIs, connections,
   and model choice reduce lock-in and support complex environments.
5. **Deployment flexibility.** Organizations can deploy in the environments
   required by their architecture, policy, and risk profile.
6. **Enterprise control.** Access control, SSO, auditability, governance, and
   security are part of the platform story rather than an afterthought.
7. **Human oversight for AI.** Agents should be shown working within policies,
   permissions, approvals, and accountable operational processes.
8. **Designed for real operations.** Demonstrate requests, approvals, cases,
   incidents, assets, audits, inspections, and other concrete workflows.

## Proof and evidence

Known customer context includes the U.S. Space Force, American Express, and
Saab. Together they signal relevance to government and defence, regulated
financial services, and complex aerospace and engineering environments.

The common product pattern reported across these customers is private data made
available to end users through a secure, purpose-built interface, which can be
a web app or an agent. The interaction often includes a request-and-approval
mechanism. Treat this as the strongest unifying customer insight. Public
descriptions of the underlying data, interface, users, security model, or
customer relationship still require explicit approval.

Before publication, confirm the approved public form of every customer name,
logo, quotation, metric, case study, and relationship claim. Do not infer that a
logo is approved merely because it exists in an asset library or draft page.

Prefer evidence that explains:

- The operational problem.
- What the customer built or improved.
- Why governance, security, deployment, or extensibility mattered.
- The measurable or observable result.

A smaller amount of meaningful proof is stronger than an unexplained logo wall.

## Brand personality

Budibase is:

- **Direct:** Lead with the operational point and use concrete language.
- **Capable:** Demonstrate how the system works and support claims with proof.
- **Composed:** Communicate confidence without hype, fear, or excessive urgency.
- **Technical:** Respect IT audiences and provide architecture-level substance.
- **Practical:** Show real workflows, constraints, decisions, and outcomes.
- **Bold:** Make strong, memorable statements where the evidence supports them.
- **Open:** Reinforce ownership, extensibility, choice, and interoperability.

Budibase is not:

- A playful, lightweight no-code toy.
- A generic workplace productivity suite.
- A traditional, slow, or opaque enterprise software vendor.
- An AI product built around spectacle or autonomous-agent hype.
- A developer tool that ignores operators, governance, or business outcomes.

## Design principles

### Built with precision

Use disciplined grids, clear hierarchy, deliberate spacing, concise copy, and
well-structured technical information. The experience should feel engineered.

### Bold, never noisy

Use large condensed typography and strong brand color for decisive moments.
Create emphasis through scale and restraint. Do not allow decorative variety to
compete with meaning.

### Show the system working

Prefer product interfaces, workflow sequences, architecture diagrams, data
flows, approvals, audit trails, and operational states over abstract decoration.
Visuals should help a viewer understand what happens, who remains in control,
and how the system responds.

### Make control visible

Security, governance, permissions, deployment, observability, and human
oversight should appear throughout the story. Do not isolate trust on a single
security page.

### Earn enterprise confidence with evidence

Use customer outcomes, product detail, architecture, standards, and specific
capabilities. Avoid relying on conservative styling alone to imply maturity.

## Visual direction

### Color

- Keep forest green as the primary brand anchor. It communicates ownership,
  control, stability, and a distinctive Budibase identity.
- Use bright celery to signal action, progress, connection, and successful
  system states.
- Give supporting colors defined semantic roles, such as agents, automations,
  apps, data, alerts, or approvals.
- Avoid rainbow palettes without meaning, fashionable gradients used only for
  novelty, and excessive color variation across adjacent sections.

### Typography

- Use the condensed display face for major statements, category claims, and
  moments that should feel unmistakably Budibase.
- Use the primary sans-serif for explanation, evidence, technical detail, and
  interface content.
- Avoid turning every heading into an oversized all-caps statement. Contrast is
  what gives the display typography authority.

### Layout

- Use clear grids, strong alignment, generous but controlled space, and modular
  compositions.
- Balance large brand statements with denser sections containing product and
  technical evidence.
- Rounded cards and pills should have a functional reason. Avoid a soft,
  consumer-SaaS appearance created by rounding every surface.

### Imagery and product presentation

Prioritize:

- Real product screens at a legible scale.
- End-to-end operational workflows.
- Architecture and deployment diagrams.
- Connections between agents, automations, apps, data, and people.
- Authentic customer environments and stories where available.

Use lifestyle photography sparingly. Do not use generic office imagery as a
substitute for showing the operational system.

### Motion

Use motion to explain cause and effect: a request arrives, policy is checked, an
agent acts, a person approves, systems update, and the audit record is retained.
Avoid decorative motion that makes the platform feel less predictable or
controlled.

## Messaging system

### Message hierarchy

Organize customer-facing narratives in this order when appropriate:

1. The important operational outcome.
2. Who the platform is for and why the work is difficult.
3. How Budibase combines apps, automations, agents, data, and oversight.
4. How IT retains control through security, governance, deployment, and
   extensibility.
5. Evidence from customers, the product, architecture, or measurable outcomes.
6. A clear next action.

### Preferred language

Use language such as:

- Build and run critical operations.
- Operational systems and operational workflows.
- Control, ownership, governance, auditability, and human oversight.
- Deploy on your terms.
- Connect existing systems and data.
- Extend the platform.
- Reliable, secure, accountable, and production-ready.
- Apps, automations, agents, and data working together.

### Language to handle carefully

- **No-code/low-code:** Use when necessary for product clarity or search intent,
  but do not make it the primary enterprise identity.
- **AI-powered/AI-first:** Explain the operational job, controls, and outcome
  instead of using AI as an unsupported value claim.
- **Build anything:** Replace with concrete operational use cases.
- **Democratize:** State who can do what, under which controls.
- **Magic, effortless, revolutionary:** Replace hype with evidence.
- **Digital transformation:** Prefer a specific operational change or outcome.

### Voice

- Write short, declarative headlines.
- Use precise nouns and active verbs.
- Explain complex capabilities in plain language without removing technical
  substance.
- Prefer confidence over superlatives.
- Match every major claim with product detail, architecture, customer proof, or
  another credible reason to believe.

## Reference brands

Use references as lenses, not templates:

- **Grafana — primary brand reference:** Technical credibility, open-source to
  enterprise progression, extensibility, practitioner trust, and an ability to
  serve individual users and major organizations without splitting the brand.
- **Atlassian — secondary platform reference:** Clear organization of a broad
  product portfolio, enterprise navigation, and platform-level storytelling.
- **Dropbox — selective editorial reference:** Typographic confidence,
  simplicity, and memorable visual restraint.
- **Slack — selective composition reference:** Clear hero construction,
  customer proof, and understandable product demonstrations.
- **Rippling — selective commercial reference:** Upmarket conversion discipline
  and confident sales journeys, without adopting its glossy or lifestyle-led
  art direction.

The intended combination is:

> Grafana's technical credibility + Atlassian's platform clarity + Budibase's
> distinctive operational identity.

Do not copy another company's palette, typography, illustration system, layout,
or tone closely enough to weaken Budibase's own identity.

## Channel application

### Website

Lead with operational outcomes, show the platform working, make enterprise
control visible, and provide evidence early. Preserve a coherent journey from
category and audience through capability, trust, proof, and action.

### Campaigns and product launches

Anchor the campaign in a specific operational problem. Explain what changed,
how the system works, why it is safe to adopt, and what outcome is now possible.

### Customer stories

Center the customer's operational environment, constraints, decision, build,
governance requirements, and outcome. Avoid turning stories into feature lists.

### Sales enablement

Equip champions to explain both sides of the value: operational adaptability
and IT control. Anticipate security, deployment, governance, ownership,
integration, and scale questions.

### Executive communications

Focus on operational resilience, speed of change, risk reduction, ownership,
and the ability to improve critical processes without an expanding custom
software burden.

## Recommended homepage narrative

1. The open-source platform for critical enterprise operations.
2. Recognizable and approved customer proof.
3. Private data made useful through governed web apps and agents.
4. Show a request entering, permissions being checked, approval occurring, and
   the authorized result or action reaching the end user.
5. Apps, automations, agents, data, and oversight as one operational system.
6. Architecture, deployment, connection, and extensibility choices.
7. Security, governance, reliability, and human oversight.
8. Customer outcomes from regulated or complex environments.
9. A clear enterprise next step.

## Guardrails

- Do not fabricate or overstate customer relationships, compliance status,
  certifications, performance, security guarantees, or product capabilities.
- Distinguish current capabilities from roadmap direction.
- Do not use regulated-enterprise anxiety as fear-based marketing.
- Do not remove the open-source identity in pursuit of enterprise seriousness.
- Do not make the brand so conservative that it becomes interchangeable with
  legacy enterprise software.
- Do not let visual novelty obscure product understanding.

## Open questions

Maintain these as explicit questions until decisions or evidence resolve them:

- Which headline or tagline is approved for long-term category ownership?
- Which customer names, logos, stories, and metrics are approved for public use?
- Which regulated industries should receive dedicated vertical messaging first?
- Which enterprise security and governance capabilities are most decisive in
  successful evaluations?
- Which operational workflows provide the strongest repeatable product proof?
- Which private data sources, end-user roles, requests, approval steps, and
  permitted actions can be shown publicly in a representative workflow?
- What quantitative outcomes can Budibase substantiate consistently?
